# Educational plan

## Verifiable promise
When finished, the student can turn a recurring decision into well-scoped questions, recognize when evidence is missing, design a review policy, and calculate whether automation is worth it. In the technical track, they can specify and evaluate an integration without confusing a demonstration with a production result.

After completing the course, the student can turn a recurring decision into well-defined questions, recognize when evidence is missing, design a review policy, and calculate whether automation is worth it. In the technical learning track, they can specify and evaluate an integration without confusing a demonstration with a production result.

## Audience and prerequisites

Proposed main audience: operations professionals, educators, and automation creators, including beginners. Language in Portuguese, with service and everyday administration examples. Do not assume the student knows JSON or probability.

Track 1: reading texts and basic computer use. Track 2: process decisions and simple spreadsheet; exercises possible on paper. Track 3: JSON, terminal, and notions of Python/API; offer short preparation with a glossary and commented payload before the labs.

## Path

| Track | Modules | Estimated time | Outcome |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 1 — understand | 1–4 | 4 h | Distinguish classification, generation, rules, and confidence |
| 2 — apply | 5–8 | 6 h | Design decisions in the ten cases and a review policy |
| 3 — build and evaluate | 9–12 | 8 h | Specify integration, benchmark, and an operations plan |

The 18 h include reading, exercises, and a project. This is an estimate to adjust during the test with students, not the duration of already-produced video content. No-code path: tracks 1–2 and a spreadsheet project. Technical path: all tracks and a runnable project when the lab exists.

## Observable objectives

1. Given a problem, justify a rule, Choice, Noul, Score, or an LLM.
2. Write distinguishable options, criteria, and an insufficient-condition.
3. Separate model confidence from statistical evidence of correctness.
4. Design review and overlapping routes without granting the model permissions.
5. Fix the video cost calculation and include fallback and human work.
6. Plan a comparison without data leakage and with results per class.
7. Explain a high-confidence error and propose the smallest correction to the flow.

## Teaching strategy

Each class: concrete situation → concept in plain language → solved example → common mistake → exercise → correction criterion → link to the next class. Start with an inbox, not the supplier’s marketing. Introduce technical terms after showing the problem they solve.

Continuous case: an ARCKHÉ team receives access requests, billing, and sales information. With each track, the student improves triage, without sending real messages or moving money.

## API dependency

Conceptual learning does not require an account or credits. The material should provide invented examples labeled as simulation and spreadsheet activities. Real exercises are clearly separated and depend on access. Simulated results never feed Jev performance leaderboards.
