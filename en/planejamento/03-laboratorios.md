# Labs and evaluation

All datasets and answer keys below are **planned deliverables**. Use fictional data in the public materials; operational data stays in the application project with its own controls.

| Lab | Material to produce | Exercise | Completion evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 — formulate | 12 everyday situations | Choose a rule, Choice, Noul, Score, or generation | Justified response and commented answer key |
| L2 — hotel | 6 fictitious cancellation policies | Separate money, credit, conflict, and omission | No absence treated automatically as “no” |
| L3 — support | 30 fictitious tickets in Portuguese | Label a queue and sufficiency | Manual matrix with explained divergences |
| L4 — evidence | 8 pairs of claim/passage and 4 fictitious clauses | Mark support and pending review | Evidence excerpt and the limit of the conclusion |
| L5 — routes | 12 tasks with a catalog of abstract models/agents | Choose a path and a attempts cap | Diagram without unlimited loops or implicit permissions |
| L6 — DOM | 5 textual lists of elements | Choose a candidate or abstain | Distinguish semantic selection from click execution |
| L7 — economics | Editable spreadsheet and scenarios | Calculate API, fallback, and review | Corrected account and sensitivity analysis |
| L8 — evaluation | Simulated predictions with high-confidence error | Measure error and coverage while varying a threshold | A report labeled as simulation, without claiming benchmark performance |

## Final project

Problem: a team receives tickets and needs to suggest queues. Provide a taxonomy; five difficult examples; atomic questions; review policy; flow diagram; data and evaluation plan; budget; operations and return criteria.

No-code path delivers a spreadsheet and specification. Technical path adds execution in the application repository’s lab when implemented, anonymized logs, and a reproducible report. If there is no real access, the execution uses a fixture labeled as such; do not invent successful calls.

## 100-point grading rubric

| Criterion | Points | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Formulation and options | 20 | Specific questions, without improper overlap, insufficient data handled |
| Decision policy | 20 | Consequences, review, allowed actions, and repetition limits |
| Evidence and quality | 25 | Reviewed reference, separation of data, and per-class errors |
| Economics and latency | 15 | Correct calculation and costs of the full flow |
| Reproducibility and communication | 20 | Versions, sources, procedure, and the distinction between simulation and real results |

Proposed approval: 75 points and correction of essential failures. Claiming that confidence guarantees truth, presenting a simulation as a benchmark, or allowing an action outside the policy requires a project review, regardless of the summed total.

## Exit questions and expected response

1. “If there are only two options, the model can’t be wrong?” You can still choose the wrong alternative.
2. “Does Noul 0,05 mean low confidence?” It means low probability of sim, not a confidence field.
3. “Does lack of confirmation mean no scheduling took place?” Not necessarily; preserve insufficiency.
4. “Do 10 thousand tokens cost US$ 0,0042 on that tariff?” No; US$ 0,00042.
5. “Does the external benchmark validate support in Portuguese?” No; we need evaluation in the target domain.
6. “Can I send a screenshot directly?” The documented modality is text; it requires a previous step.
7. “If I chose the financial agent: can I pay?” Route choice does not grant permission.
8. “If the pilot doesn’t save money, did the project fail?” A well-founded decision not to adopt is a useful outcome.
